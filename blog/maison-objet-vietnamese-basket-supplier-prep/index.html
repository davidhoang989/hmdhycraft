Maison&Objet prep for a Vietnamese basket supplier — what to bring, how to book buyers, what closes deals

31 July 2026·10 min read·Last updated 31 July 2026

Maison&Objet Paris is the highest-value B2B home décor trade show in Europe, and one of the biggest single-event exposures a Vietnamese handwoven basket exporter can get to European retail buyers. The show is expensive, tiring, and works differently from Vietnamese domestic trade shows in almost every practical way. This piece is the full preparation playbook — for exporters exhibiting for the first time, and for European buyers who want to know what to expect when meeting Vietnamese suppliers there.

Written for Vietnamese handwoven basket exporters planning their first Maison&Objet appearance, and for European retail buyers preparing show-week meetings with Vietnamese suppliers. Covers booth economics, sample transport, meeting scheduling through M&O's own booking platform, on-booth conversation flow, and the 4-week follow-up cadence that converts show contacts into first orders.

Booth economics for a first-time Vietnamese exhibitor

Booth space rents from €340 to €520 per square metre depending on hall location, edition (January is slightly cheaper than September), and how early the booking is made. A typical Vietnamese basket exporter booth is 12 to 18 square metres — big enough to display 25-40 SKUs on wall shelving and 6-10 hero pieces on floor plinths, small enough to be commercially manageable at first-exhibitor budget.

ROI break-even: 2 to 3 confirmed first orders from show contacts, each landing €4,000-8,000 FOB. That is a realistic target for a well-prepared first-time exhibitor with genuine differentiated product. Exhibitors who go with an unfocused catalogue or unmemorable booth typically do not clear the break-even bar on the first appearance and need to iterate.

Sample transport — the ATA Carnet approach

Two options for getting basket samples from HCMC to Paris:

Standard air freight through Paris CDG. Vietnamese exporter's forwarder ships samples 5-10 days before show setup, cleared through French customs at CDG. Reliable but samples are treated as commercial import — French duty and VAT payable on the CIF value of the samples (typically €400-800 depending on sample volume). Duty and VAT recoverable at end of show only if samples re-export to Vietnam under formal procedure.

ATA Carnet (recommended). International customs document allowing temporary import of samples without payment of duty or VAT at destination. Samples must return to origin country (Vietnam) after the show, OR be sold under proper import formality after the show. HMD's forwarder handles ATA Carnet issuance through the Vietnamese Chamber of Commerce and Industry (VCCI); one-time paperwork setup, valid for one year and multiple shows.

Recommendation for first-time exhibitors: ATA Carnet. Saves €400-800 in immediate customs cost, simplifies post-show sample handling (unsold samples ship back to Vietnam under Carnet; sold samples get proper import invoice generated in follow-up).

Meeting scheduling — the My M&O platform

Maison&Objet runs a buyer-exhibitor matching platform called My M&O. Serious buyers use it heavily to plan show routes 3-6 weeks in advance. Vietnamese suppliers should treat My M&O as the primary lead-generation channel, not a nice-to-have.

Practical workflow, 8 weeks to show:

  1. 1.8 weeks: Complete My M&O exhibitor profile fully. Upload SKU catalogue (20-40 SKUs), high-res photos, brand story, MOQ information, incoterm capability. Incomplete profiles rank lower in buyer search.
  2. 2.6 weeks: Filter the buyer database by relevance (buyer role, category interest, country of operation). Build a target list of 200-400 European buyers who match the exhibitor's programme (retail chains, independent boutiques, e-commerce brands, hotel groups).
  3. 3.5 weeks: Send personalised meeting requests through My M&O to each target buyer. Standard cold-outreach template — reference the buyer's country and category, mention one specific SKU that would fit their programme, propose a 20-minute meeting during show days.
  4. 4.3 weeks: Follow up on unreplied requests once. Confirm meetings that have been accepted with a specific time slot.
  5. 5.1 week: Send a "see you at Booth X" reminder to all confirmed meetings.

Realistic conversion: 15-25% of cold-outreach requests convert to accepted meetings for a first-time Vietnamese exhibitor. From 200-400 outreach messages, expect 30-100 confirmed meetings across the 5 show days. That is a realistic target — enough meetings to fill the booth schedule without burnout, enough surface area for genuine sales conversations to develop.

What actually closes deals in the booth

Two very different things: what fills the booth, and what closes deals in the booth.

What fills the booth (attention capture)

  • Distinctive product photography visible from 10 metres away. The backdrop wall on the booth is a giant billboard — buyers walk past thousands of booths per day and only stop at the ones whose visual language stands out. Bland "generic Vietnamese basket" photography does not stop anyone.
  • Well-lit product wall. Show halls are dimly lit; well-lit booths draw the eye. Invest in the fitted-booth lighting package.
  • Clearly signposted brand story. "HMD Strategy & Export · Water hyacinth & seagrass baskets · Mekong Delta, Vietnam" in large clear typography at eye level tells the walking buyer what the booth offers in 3 seconds.
  • One or two eye-catching hero pieces on floor plinths — an oversized floor basket, a distinctive coloured piece, a private-label reference sample. Gives the buyer a specific talking point.

What closes deals in the booth (conversation quality)

  • Knowledgeable Director or founder physically present. Buyers refuse to book first orders with junior sales staff. Have decision-makers on the booth for the full 5 days, not just the opening day.
  • Physical samples the buyer can touch and stress-test. Buyers pick up baskets, test weight, feel weave density, tug at handles. Every sample on the booth must be a real production piece — not a marketing prototype.
  • Honest answers to MOQ and lead-time questions. Buyers ask MOQ, lead time, and payment terms in the first 3 minutes of every meeting. Vague or evasive answers signal amateur operations; specific numbers signal commercial competence.
  • Printed catalogue with pricing NOT visible on the buyer-facing pages. Standard practice in the sector — HMD's catalogue lists SKU codes, dimensions, materials, MOQ, and incoterm but never lists prices. Pricing is quoted after buyer specifies items, in the follow-up email post-show.

The single most valuable booth investment

A high-quality printed booklet-format catalogue that the buyer physically takes with them at the end of the meeting. 40-60 SKUs, hi-res photos, dimensions, materials, MOQ, HMD brand story, contact information for follow-up. Buyers physically carry the catalogues home and reference them when discussing suppliers with their internal team over the following weeks. Digital catalogues (PDF sent via email post-show) are forgotten within days; physical catalogues sit on the buyer's desk. Cost per catalogue: €4-8 with high-quality print. Print 200 for a first show; 300-400 for a second.

The 4-week follow-up cadence

Buyers meet 40+ suppliers per day at Maison&Objet. The supplier who follows up within 48 hours with the specific quote referenced in conversation wins the first order. Supplier who follows up in week 3 is competing against 40 other suppliers with equal claim; supplier who follows up on Day 2 is competing against 2-3.

The buyer's perspective — what European buyers expect from Vietnamese suppliers at Maison&Objet

For European retail buyers preparing to meet Vietnamese basket suppliers at the show, here is what a well-organised Vietnamese exhibitor looks like:

  • English-language communication throughout. Fluent business English from the founder or Director on the booth. If the primary contact needs a translator, treat that as a signal about ongoing communication ease.
  • Physical samples of real production quality — not prototypes or marketing-only pieces. Ask to inspect one carton of pre-shipment production stock to verify.
  • Specific MOQ, lead time, and incoterm numbers. Not "flexible" or "negotiable" — actual numbers backed by production capacity.
  • Verifiable references from existing European buyers. Ask for two references you can contact independently.
  • Certificate of origin capability (EUR.1 for EU buyers). Confirms the supplier is REX-registered and can deliver EVFTA zero-duty paperwork.
  • A clear brand story explaining who they are, where they are, and what they do. Vague suppliers ("we can do everything, from ceramics to furniture to baskets") almost always deliver a diluted, unremarkable product.
Maison&Objet is not where deals close. It is where deals start.

The best first orders that come out of a Maison&Objet meeting typically ship 4 to 6 months after the show — after samples, video calls, sample-refinement rounds, and final PO negotiation. Suppliers who expect to close deals at the booth are usually disappointed; suppliers who expect to start 30-100 new buyer conversations that convert to 5-15 first orders over the following 6-9 months typically hit their ROI target.

Frequently asked questions

When is Maison&Objet and how many editions per year?

Two editions: January (week 3-4) and September (week 1-2), both 5 days at Paris Nord Villepinte. ~60,000 buyers, ~2,500 exhibitors.

What does a Maison&Objet booth cost for a Vietnamese exporter?

All-in first-time budget €16,850-25,450 including 12-18 m² space, fitted booth, sample shipping, flights, hotel, catalogue. ROI break-even at 2-3 confirmed first orders.

How should a Vietnamese supplier transport samples to Paris?

ATA Carnet recommended — temporary import without French duty/VAT. HMD's forwarder handles VCCI Carnet issuance. Alternative: standard air freight through CDG with €400-800 duty cost.

How do European buyers actually schedule meetings at Maison&Objet?

Through My M&O platform, 3-6 weeks in advance. Vietnamese suppliers should send 200-400 cold-outreach messages; realistic 15-25% conversion = 30-100 confirmed meetings.

What closes deals at Maison&Objet vs what fills the room?

Fills room: distinctive photography, well-lit product wall, clear brand story. Closes deals: knowledgeable Director present, physical samples to touch, specific MOQ/lead-time answers, fast 48-hour follow-up.

What is the follow-up cadence after a Maison&Objet meeting?

48h thank-you email + week 1-2 physical samples + week 2-4 video call + week 4-8 Proforma. Month 3 re-engagement for dormant leads recovers ~20%.
