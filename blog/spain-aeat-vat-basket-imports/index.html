Spain basket imports — AEAT VAT, Barcelona/Valencia ports, Spanish-language paperwork

31 July 2026·9 min read·Last updated 31 July 2026

Spain is one of the largest European destinations for Vietnamese handwoven basketwork after the UK and Germany, driven by the Spanish gift-hamper (cesta) sector and the coastal-décor retail market. The customs mechanics are straightforward under EVFTA — zero duty into Spain from Vietnam, 21 percent IVA on top. The details worth knowing are the AEAT declaration through the DUA system, the port choice between Barcelona and Valencia, and the Spanish-language paperwork practicalities that make a Spanish buyer's finance team happier without changing the customs outcome.

Written for Spanish-based buyers importing handwoven baskets from Vietnam, and for buyers in Portugal, France, and Italy using Spanish ports as their EU entry point. Covers AEAT, IVA, EVFTA preferential duty, DUA declaration workflow, and the timing considerations for peak-season gift-hamper programmes.

The AEAT declaration mechanics

AEAT (Agencia Estatal de Administración Tributaria) is Spain's tax authority, administering both customs and IVA (Spanish VAT). All commercial imports into Spain are declared to AEAT through the DUA (Documento Único Administrativo) — Spain's version of the EU single administrative document, filed electronically through AEAT's ADT platform by the buyer's Spanish customs broker.

Filed correctly with valid EUR.1, AEAT assesses zero duty and 21 percent IVA on the customs value. Container releases to the buyer's inland transport once IVA is settled (either paid at declaration or deferred via monthly aplazamiento arrangement).

EVFTA zero duty — the same mechanism as Germany

The EU-Vietnam FTA covers Spain equally with all other EU member states. HS 4602 handwoven basketwork clears at 0 percent duty (versus 3.7 percent MFN) with valid EVFTA proof of origin. Two mechanisms:

  • EUR.1 certificate from Vietnamese MoIT via eCoSys for shipments above €6,000.
  • Exporter self-declaration on invoice for shipments up to €6,000, if exporter is REX-registered.

HMD is REX-registered and issues EUR.1 on every Spanish-bound shipment above €6,000, self-declaration below. The Spanish broker files under preference code 300 with document code N954. Zero duty applied automatically at DUA acceptance.

IVA — the 21% VAT and how it flows through

IVA at 21 percent applies to handwoven basket imports as the standard Spanish VAT rate. Reduced IVA rates (10% for cultural goods, 4% for essentials) do not apply to home décor basketwork.

IVA is paid at DUA acceptance and reclaimed on the buyer's next Modelo 303 VAT declaration (quarterly for most importers, monthly for larger taxpayers). Cash flow implication: buyers running multiple containers per quarter have €5,000-€15,000 tied up in the IVA reclaim cycle at any time.

Two mechanisms to defer or eliminate the cash impact:

  • Aplazamiento del IVA a la importación — Spain's deferred import IVA scheme. Under this scheme (available since 2015), IVA is not paid at DUA but declared on the same Modelo 303 as reclaimable — net zero cash impact. Requires AEAT authorisation; standard for volume importers, straightforward to apply.
  • Section 21 EU VAT deferral via Dutch fiscal representative in Rotterdam. Containers routed through Rotterdam with a Dutch VAT-deferral setup skip Spanish import IVA entirely at the border, with IVA settled at the domestic supply stage in Spain. Cost-effective for buyers running high shipment values who do not have Aplazamiento authorisation set up.

Valencia is slightly cheaper on FCL freight and slightly faster on ocean transit. Barcelona has stronger rail connectivity for onward distribution to France and Italy. For a buyer whose Spanish DC is in Barcelona metropolitan area, Barcelona port is the obvious choice; for a buyer in Madrid or south of Madrid, Valencia is usually preferred. Algeciras in Andalusia is a smaller third option for buyers in southern Spain or with onward North Africa distribution.

Spanish-language paperwork — where it matters and where it does not

Common misconception: Spanish AEAT requires Spanish-language commercial invoices. Not true. AEAT accepts English-language invoices (standard international trade practice), and the DUA translates through commodity code and standardised customs data fields.

Where Spanish-language documentation adds value for the Spanish buyer (not for customs, but for buyer's operational workflow):

  • Bilingual English-Spanish commercial invoice simplifies the buyer's accounting team reconciliation, particularly for buyers not fluent in commercial English. HMD provides bilingual format on request at no additional cost.
  • Spanish-language packing list for buyers whose DC receiving team operates in Spanish. HMD provides bilingual packing list on request.
  • Retail hangtag with Spanish-language product description for products intended for direct-to-consumer sale in Spanish retail. Not a customs requirement (English "Made in Vietnam" is legally sufficient for country-of-origin), but a retail-conversion requirement. HMD prints hangtags in buyer-specified language at packing — typical additional cost €0.02-0.04 per unit.
  • User care instructions in Spanish if the retail SKU is sold to Spanish consumers with a care instruction card. Same mechanism as hangtag: HMD prints in buyer-specified language.

Spanish gift-hamper sector — the Christmas timing

Spain's cesta (gift hamper) sector is a substantial buyer of Vietnamese basketwork. Cestas de Navidad (Christmas hampers) are a deeply embedded corporate gift tradition — Spanish employers commonly give a hamper to every employee before Christmas, filled with wine, jamón, turrón, and gourmet food items. Baskets used as the hamper carrier are almost entirely handwoven, and the volume peaks between late September and mid-November for retail assembly.

Early-June PO is the safety margin.

The Spanish-specific detail worth confirming

Confirm your Spanish broker files under preference code 300 (EVFTA) and document code N954 (EUR.1). Some Spanish brokers unfamiliar with EVFTA still default-file under MFN (preference code 100) which loses the zero-duty saving. HMD's commercial invoice always references EVFTA and the EUR.1 number to make the correct filing obvious. If the broker files under MFN by default, a post-clearance amendment recovers the duty but adds 60-90 days of administrative work.

Frequently asked questions

What is AEAT and how does it govern basket imports?

Spain's tax authority, administering customs and IVA. Imports declared through DUA electronic system. IVA and duty payable before container release.

What is the current IVA rate on handwoven baskets in Spain?

Standard 21% on CIF+duty. Reduced (10%) and super-reduced (4%) rates do not apply. Reclaimable by VAT-registered businesses via Modelo 303.

Do I need a Spanish NIF or EORI to import?

Yes both. Spanish importers register NIF + ES-EORI through AEAT. EU importers can use existing EU EORI. Non-EU importers need Spanish fiscal representative.

Which Spanish port is best for basket imports from Vietnam?

Valencia slightly cheaper + faster transit. Barcelona better rail connectivity. Choose based on inland distribution — Barcelona for northern Spain, Valencia for central/south.

Does the commercial invoice need to be in Spanish?

No. English acceptable for AEAT. Bilingual English-Spanish available from HMD on request for buyer's finance team convenience.

What is the timeline from HCMC to a Spanish DC?

11-14 weeks PO-to-DC first shipment. 6-8 weeks production + 4-6 days origin customs + 32-40 days ocean transit + 2-4 days clearance + 1-3 days inland.
